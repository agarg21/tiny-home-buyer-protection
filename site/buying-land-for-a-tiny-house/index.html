Buying Land for a Tiny House? Check the Parcel Before You Offer

Do not treat “unrestricted,” “tiny-home friendly,” or “utilities nearby” as proof. Before money is at risk, retain evidence for the exact parcel and government, intended home and use, legal access and restrictions, wastewater and water, utilities, and site constraints.

This page does not list land. Use it after finding a parcel or listing. It organizes evidence; it does not decide legality, approval, or buildability.

Four things to name before you rely on a listing

A mailing address, seller label, or county name can point to the wrong rules. Tie every later answer to the same parcel, authority, home, and intended use.

  2. 2
    Controlling government

    Identify the incorporated city, unincorporated county, or other authority that controls this parcel.

  3. 3
    Home category

    Name the actual foundation dwelling, ADU, manufactured home, movable tiny house, park model, or RV path.

    Intended use

    State whether the plan is primary, accessory, rental, or temporary/RV occupancy.

Translate sales shorthand into evidence requests

Listing phrases that prove very little

These phrases may describe a real advantage. They are not enough to risk earnest money without the underlying parcel-specific evidence.

“Unrestricted”

Ask which recorded restrictions were searched, whether an HOA or architectural review exists, and which public zoning and building rules still apply.

“Tiny-home friendly”

Ask which exact home category and intended use were discussed with which controlling office, for this parcel, and when.

“Utilities nearby”

Ask the serving provider for written availability, extension responsibility, easements, capacity, and unresolved construction.

“Road access”

Ask for recorded legal access and maintenance terms, then separately verify delivery, utility, construction, and emergency use.

“Perc available”

Ask for the actual report, parcel match, date, proposed system, current local requirements, and what design or approval remains.

“Owner financing”

Review price, title, lien position, default terms, balloon payment, due diligence, cancellation, and professional/legal review before relying on easy financing.
