Before the placement check

Identify what you are actually buying

"Tiny house" is a marketing term, not one universal legal category. Ask the seller for the documents or labels that show which path the home was built for.

Movable

Tiny house on wheels

May be reviewed as an RV, movable tiny house, accessory unit, or another local category. Ask what certification, plans, and inspection path apply.

Recreation / park

RV or park model

Often designed for a park, campground, or community. Full-time residential use can be a separate question from where the unit may be parked.

Built on site

Foundation home or ADU

Usually follows a residential or accessory-dwelling permit path. The lot, primary-home context, building code, utilities, and intended occupancy still matter.

Factory built

Modular or manufactured

Certification and installation rules can differ from both site-built homes and RVs. Confirm the label and regulatory program before discussing placement.

Before a deposit or land offer

Placement checklist: get the evidence in writing

First confirm the parcel number and governing office using the parcel record or planning office. A mailing address does not establish jurisdiction.

Send the office the parcel details, unit plans or labels, and intended use. Ask:

For parcel [parcel number] in [jurisdiction], how do you classify [unit and label] for [intended use], and which zoning, occupancy, building, and site approvals must I obtain before purchase?

Keep the written response and unresolved questions with your purchase documents. Verbal reassurance is hard to use when an office, seller, lender, or insurer later disagrees.

  1. Parcel authority: exact jurisdiction, parcel number, zoning district, overlays, and governing office.
  2. Home category: plans, labels, certification, build standard, and the local office's classification.
  3. Use and occupancy: whether the intended primary, accessory, rental, temporary, or park use can be reviewed on that property.
  4. Site work: access, fire, foundation or anchoring, delivery, septic, water, power, grading, and inspections.
  5. Seller scope: what the price includes, excludes, delivers, warrants, and requires someone else to complete.
  6. Private constraints: HOA, deed, lease, lender, and insurer requirements before money becomes nonrefundable.
