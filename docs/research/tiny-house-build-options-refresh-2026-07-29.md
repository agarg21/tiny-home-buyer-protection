## Decision

Build one national comparison and due-diligence page for people choosing among
plans, kits, shells, a trailer/THOW build, a custom tiny-home builder, a
conventional ADU contractor, and a park-model/RV-style product.

directory. Its useful gap is neutral help comparing which party owns design,
structure, systems, inspections, delivery, and site work before a buyer

## Representative Ranking Pages

### Plan inventory

Houseplans.com's tiny-house collection provides filters, dimensions, room
counts, and plan inventory. That is useful when the reader is already shopping
for drawings, but it does not solve the neutral build-path decision. Tiny Home
Clarity has neither the inventory nor the authority to imitate it.

### Builder directory

TinyHouse.com's directory organizes builder contacts by region and category.
That satisfies provider-discovery intent, but a directory would create
vetting, freshness, recommendation, and lead-generation obligations that are
outside this transaction.

### Vendor kit guides

Vendor-authored kit guides often compare panelized packages, shells, SIPs, and
turnkey products. They show that "kit" is used loosely, but their price,
timeline, completeness, and performance claims are commercial assertions. This
page should ask for the included components, engineering, labor, tools,
delivery, unloading, setup, and correction responsibility instead.

### Community questions

Community discussions repeatedly expose four useful failure modes:

- Plans are mistaken for step-by-step construction instructions.
- Kits and shells are assumed to include systems or completion work that was
  never in the written scope.
- Road and trailer documentation is mistaken for permission to occupy or place
  the home.
- Buyers ask for trusted builders before they have a contract, proof, or
  verification framework.

These discussions are qualitative evidence, not measured prevalence.

## Source Review

### International Code Council

Source:
`https://codes.iccsafe.org/content/THPOTIRC2021P1`

The 2021 International Tiny House Provisions collect model-code paths and
standards relevant to some tiny-house categories. A model code is not national
permission, and local adoption and project acceptance vary. The page may use
this source to explain why a seller should identify the proposed standard and
why the local office must still confirm the path.

### HUD manufactured-home program

Source:
`https://www.hud.gov/hud-partners/manufactured-home-resources`

HUD describes federal certification labels and homeowner resources for
manufactured homes. This does not make a generic tiny house a HUD Code home.
The page should ask for the actual label and program rather than borrow
"manufactured" language.

### NHTSA trailer manufacturer and VIN guidance

Source:
`https://www.nhtsa.gov/importing-vehicle/importation-and-certification-faqs-1`

NHTSA explains manufacturer, VIN, certification-label, and gross vehicle weight
rating responsibilities for road vehicles and trailers. Those records can
matter to a THOW purchase, but they do not establish residential occupancy or
placement approval.

### Federal Trade Commission contractor guidance

Source:
`https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/how-avoid-home-improvement-scam`

FTC guidance supports checking license and insurance where applicable, getting
multiple written estimates, using a written contract, and avoiding pressure
for full upfront payment. State and local contractor rules still vary.

### RV Industry Association standards and intended use

Sources:

- `https://www.rvia.org/standards-regulations/association-and-ansi-adopted-standards`
- `https://www.rvia.org/advocacy/policies/what-rv`

RVIA identifies NFPA 1192 for RVs and ANSI A119.5 for park-model RVs and
describes these products as recreational, seasonal, or temporary rather than
permanent residences. This is attributed industry guidance, not government
approval or a local-use determination.

## Searcher Decision Job

Primary reader: a beginner-to-intermediate buyer or would-be builder who has
encountered several products with apparently comparable price tags.

The decision job is:

> Choose a build or purchase path without accidentally accepting design,
> completion, inspection, transport, or site responsibilities that were hidden
> outside the advertised product.

The first screen must answer that job directly. It must not ask the reader to
choose an intent before giving value.

## Experience-Level Requirements

### Beginner

Needs plain definitions, a visible comparison, and an explanation that plans,
kits, shells, trailers, builders, ADU contractors, and park models are not
interchangeable products.

### Intermediate

Needs scope boundaries, handoff risks, written questions, contract terms, and
connections to placement and project-budget work.

### Advanced

Needs specific records: plan-set contents, engineering criteria, bill of
materials, substitutions, chassis and weight documents, standards and labels,
inspection records, change-order rules, correction responsibility, warranty,
and cancellation terms.

## Page Architecture

1. First-screen answer: compare responsibility before price.
2. Seven-path comparison table.
3. Explanation of why similar labels and prices can hide different projects.
4. Seven document groups to collect before paying.
5. Red flags and stop conditions.
6. Official and attributed-industry source notes with scope boundaries.
7. Internal links to the placement hub, cost calculator, and under-$20K guide.

## Claim And Product Boundaries

The page must not:

- Recommend or rank a builder, plan seller, kit seller, or product.
- Claim one path is always cheaper, faster, safer, legal, financeable, or
  insurable.
- State a typical or complete project cost.
- Treat a model code, VIN, certification label, plan set, or product standard
  as placement or occupancy approval.
- Conflate conventional ADU-contractor demand with tiny-home-builder demand.
- Answer a parcel, permit, zoning, HOA, lender, insurer, or utility decision.
