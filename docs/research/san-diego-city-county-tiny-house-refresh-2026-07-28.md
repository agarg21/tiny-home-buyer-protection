# San Diego City And County Tiny-House Refresh

Checked: 2026-07-28

Action: `THC-SD-001`

## Decision

Repair the existing San Diego page. Do not create a second local URL.

The page should answer one question before discussing listings, builders,
communities, stays, or detailed standards:

> Is the property inside the City of San Diego, in unincorporated San Diego
> County, or in another incorporated city?

There are two current, source-backed movable-tiny-house paths under the broad
"San Diego" label:

- The City of San Diego regulates a Movable Tiny House as a separately
  regulated limited use under current Municipal Code section 141.0318.
- Unincorporated San Diego County considers a qualifying Tiny Home on Wheels
  (THOW) a dwelling that may be used as a primary or accessory dwelling when
  the County's standards and permit requirements are met.

Neither source determines whether a particular parcel or unit qualifies.
Another incorporated city in San Diego County has its own rules and must not be
matched to either path.

## Official Source Review

### Determine the jurisdiction first

The County states that its land-use jurisdiction covers unincorporated land
outside the 18 incorporated cities, Tribal lands, and State and Federal land.
Its official unincorporated-area map is specifically described as a tool for
determining whether a place is in County jurisdiction.

Sources:

- [County explanation of unincorporated jurisdiction](https://engage.sandiegocounty.gov/hub-page/county-infrastructure)
- [County unincorporated-area map](https://gis-portal.sandiegocounty.gov/arcgis/home/webmap/viewer.html?webmap=61945f9d93a74a618ed55a16cffdc94c)
- [County zoning information request](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/sdc/pds/zoninghome/zoningquestions.html)
- [City zoning and ZAPP](https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/zoning)

The 18 incorporated cities are Carlsbad, Chula Vista, Coronado, Del Mar, El
Cajon, Encinitas, Escondido, Imperial Beach, La Mesa, Lemon Grove, National
City, Oceanside, Poway, San Diego, San Marcos, Santee, Solana Beach, and Vista.
The page does not need to reproduce this list above the fold. It should say
that another incorporated city is a separate path and link the County
jurisdiction tool.

### City of San Diego

Primary current source:

- [San Diego Municipal Code section 141.0318, Movable Tiny Houses](https://docs.sandiego.gov/municode/MuniCodeChapter14/Ch14Art01Division03.pdf)

The current PDF identifies itself as the July 2026 version. Section 141.0318
states that movable tiny houses are a limited use in zones marked `L` in the
applicable use-regulation tables. It includes requirements and constraints for:

- DMV licensing and registration;
- size under the public-highway movement limit;
- one story and no self-propulsion;
- right-of-way access, side/rear placement, and structure/lot-line separation;
- brush-management and MHPA exclusions;
- wheel, support-jack, and paving treatment;
- water, sewer, and electric connections, with natural gas prohibited;
- NFPA 1192 or ANSI A119.5 certification by a recognized national body;
- fire protection and Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone requirements;
- design details and coastal/sea-level-rise conditions;
- one movable tiny house per premises in addition to ADUs/JADUs allowed under
  section 141.0302; and
- no rental term shorter than 30 consecutive days.

The page should summarize the filters that change a buyer decision. It should
not reproduce the code or imply that meeting one requirement establishes
eligibility.

Supporting sources:

- [City zoning and ZAPP](https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/zoning)
- [City ADU/JADU program](https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/news-programs/programs/companion-junior-units)
- [Information Bulletin 403](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/dsdib403.pdf)

Information Bulletin 403 is dated October 2020 and describes the earlier
companion-unit framework. Its unit-count wording does not match the current
July 2026 section 141.0318 wording. The page may retain the bulletin as
historical/supporting implementation material, but current code must lead and
the old bulletin must not control current unit-count or path claims.

Source confidence: **High** for the existence and current text of the City
section 141.0318 path. Parcel and unit eligibility: **unverified**.

### Unincorporated San Diego County

Primary sources:

- [County Tiny Homes on Wheels guidance](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/sdc/pds/bldg/THOW.html)
- [PDS Director's Determination dated September 4, 2025](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/dam/sdc/pds/bldg/THOW.pdf)
- [County THOW water and wastewater FAQ, revised March 30, 2026](https://www.sandiegocounty.gov/content/dam/sdc/deh/lwqd/tinyhomesonwheelsFAQs.pdf)

The County guidance says that, since September 4, 2025, it considers a THOW a
dwelling unit under the zoning ordinance and that a qualifying THOW may be used
as a primary or accessory dwelling. A County PDS permit is required.

The current sources identify material filters including:

- unincorporated County jurisdiction;
- zoning, density, building type, setbacks, and other parcel regulations;
- ANSI A119.5 design and third-party certification;
- a published 430-square-foot cap, one story, maximum 16 feet, and a loft up
  to 150 square feet;
- DMV licensing/registration and no self-propulsion;
- fire-resistant construction, fire review, and possible sprinkler
  requirements;
- paved support, separation, utility connections, stormwater, grading, flood,
  access, right-of-way, and inspection requirements; and
- potable water and approved wastewater disposal.

The County wastewater FAQ says hauled water is not an approved potable-water
source for new construction and that composting toilets, holding tanks, and
portable toilets are not approved wastewater disposal for THOWs. It strongly
recommends obtaining environmental-health approval and discussing the project
with PDS before buying the THOW or land.

The public THOW page says a unit must be smaller than 430 square feet, while the
Director's Determination says it must not exceed 430 square feet. The page
should describe this as a published 430-square-foot cap and direct a buyer to
confirm the exact plan measurement with PDS.

The County's determination says the policy does not change allowed density or
the number of dwellings on a property. The County page also says its THOW
requirements do not apply to park trailers in mobile-home or RV parks.

Source confidence: **High** for the existence and stated scope of the County
path. Parcel, permit, water, wastewater, fire, and unit eligibility:
**unverified**.

## Searcher And Persona Strategy

### Primary decision job

Primary persona: a San Diego-area tiny-home shopper or parcel evaluator who
needs to avoid applying the wrong jurisdiction's rules before buying land,
ordering a unit, or paying a deposit.

Evidence:

- broad city and commercial demand;
- direct THOW and laws demand;
- official City and County paths with materially different uses and standards;
- forum questions that mix City, County, rural communities, empty lots,
  utilities, and permanent occupancy.

Primary failure mode: the reader treats "San Diego" as one government, relies
on a seller or generic zoning page, and buys a unit or parcel before learning
that the property is in another jurisdiction or that water, wastewater,
certification, access, density, or permit requirements block the plan.

### Beginner

Needs:

- City versus unincorporated County explained before MTH/THOW terminology;
- a direct route based on property location;
- plain-language difference between the City and County paths; and
- a concrete first action that does not require reading code.

First-screen job: choose one of three jurisdiction routes and understand that
"San Diego County" does not mean the County government controls every parcel.

### Intermediate

Needs:

- City and County comparison by intended use, unit standard, parcel context,
  utilities, and permit authority;
- protection against listing, builder, community, and stay claims;
- exact documents to request before paying; and
- a clear warning that ADU, MTH, THOW, park model, RV, and short stay are not
  interchangeable.

### Advanced

Needs:

- direct current code and determination links;
- dated source hierarchy, including the 2020 bulletin conflict;
- exact section numbers and effective/revision dates;
- unresolved density, zone, fire, access, coastal, water, wastewater, and
  certification questions; and
- the responsible City or County office for a written answer.

## Claim Boundaries

- Do not say a tiny home is legal or allowed on a particular parcel.
- Do not say all City zones permit an MTH.
- Do not say all unincorporated County lots qualify.
- Do not apply County guidance to an incorporated city, Tribal land, or State
  or Federal land.
- Do not call an MTH, THOW, park trailer, RV, conventional ADU, kit, shell,
  community, or stay interchangeable.
- Do not present the 2020 City bulletin as the current controlling source where
  it conflicts with the July 2026 code.
- Do not recommend a builder, seller, approved agency, community, or vendor.
- Do not present a quote or cost as typical or complete.
- Keep official-source confidence separate from parcel eligibility.
